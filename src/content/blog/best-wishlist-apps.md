## What Makes a Wishlist App Good?

Six criteria. I evaluated every app against all of them.

**Ease of sharing for the giver.** Can someone view and reserve without creating an account or downloading an app? This matters enormously for extended family, coworkers, and older relatives. Friction kills participation, and a wishlist nobody uses is worse than no wishlist at all.

**Surprise protection.** Can the recipient see reservations? This is the single biggest differentiator. Most apps treat the list owner as an administrator, which means they see every purchase. Fine for weddings. A disaster for birthdays.

**Duplicate prevention.** Can two people accidentally claim the same item? A good app makes duplication essentially impossible, which is half the reason to use a list in the first place.

**Group gifting.** Can multiple people chip in on one item? Important for bigger gifts — a stroller, a piece of furniture, a trip — where no single person wants to cover the full amount.

**List quality.** Is it easy to build and pleasant to browse? Mobile-first design is non-negotiable in 2027. If the app is ugly on a phone, people won't use it.

**Price.** Free is best. A reasonable subscription is fine if the app delivers. I've flagged the cost for each.

## 1. Giftly Lists (iOS) — Best for Surprise Protection

Full disclosure: I helped build this one. I built it because I couldn't find an existing app that solved the problem I had — keeping surprises intact while letting a group coordinate.

**What it does well:**

- **Hidden reservations are the core design.** The list owner sees only their items. Givers see what's been claimed. This is the one thing almost every other app gets wrong, and it's the entire reason the app exists.
- **No account required to view or reserve.** Givers open a link, see the list, tap to claim. That frictionless access is what makes it usable for grandparents and non-tech-savvy relatives.
- **Group contributions are built in.** Multiple people can chip in on a single item — a stroller, a piece of furniture, a weekend away.
- **Mobile-first.** Most wishlist apps are still designed desktop-first and it shows. This one was built for a phone from the start.
- **Free tier covers 3 lists.** Pro is $1.99/month for up to 25 lists and no ads.

**Where it's weaker:**

- **iOS only.** If your family is on Android, this is a blocker. I won't pretend otherwise.
- **No browser version.** Someone on a laptop has to use their phone.
- **Newer than the big names.** Fewer integrations, no retailer partnerships.

If surprise protection is your priority and you're an iOS household, this is the app to try. If not, keep reading — the others have real strengths.

## 2. Amazon Wishlist — Best for Pure Convenience

The most-used wishlist product in the world, for obvious reasons. If you're already buying on Amazon, adding items to a list is one tap.

**What it does well:**

- **Frictionless buying** if the giver is also on Amazon. Two taps and the gift ships.
- **Enormous catalog.** Anything you can buy on Amazon can go on the list.
- **Free and integrated** into an app most people already have installed.

**Where it falls short:**

- **The recipient sees everything.** Amazon tells you when an item has been purchased, with no way to hide it. For a birthday or holiday, that's a spoiler waiting to happen.
- **No real group gifting.** The workaround is adding a note to an item, which is clumsy.
- **Amazon-only.** Anything from a local shop, an independent artist, or a service can't be on the list.

Fine for tracking what's coming to a wedding. The wrong tool for surprise protection. My post on [why group chats and spreadsheets fail](/blog/how-to-keep-gifts-a-surprise/) explains the underlying problem.

## 3. Elfster — Best for Secret Santa

Elfster has been around for over a decade and is the best-known dedicated exchange app. It's built around gift exchanges, which is both its strength and its limitation.

**What it does well:**

- **Secret Santa is the core feature**, and it does it well. Name draws, exclusions for couples, wishlist integration.
- **Large group exchanges work smoothly.** A 30-person office exchange is well within its comfort zone.
- **Wishlist functionality is decent** — product links and notes supported.

**Where it falls short:**

- **Interface feels dated** compared to newer apps.
- **Surprise protection is partial.** The recipient can't see all reservations, but the defaults aren't built for pure surprise situations.
- **Built for exchanges, not year-round lists.**
- **Ad-supported** on the free tier.

If you're organizing a Secret Santa specifically, Elfster is a strong choice. I wrote about the Secret Santa setup in more detail in my [rules guide](/blog/secret-santa-surprise/). For general wishlist coordination, there are better options.

## 4. Giftster — Best for Large Extended Families

Giftster is designed for families coordinating across many households. More traditional in design, genuinely useful for large family networks.

**What it does well:**

- **Family groups are the core unit**, which makes multiple lists easy to manage in one place.
- **Handles "who's buying for whom"** complexity well — useful when you have twenty relatives and three gift-giving occasions per year.
- **Solid browser version**, which matters if you have relatives who don't use apps.

**Where it falls short:**

- **Design feels dated**, especially on mobile.
- **Surprise protection isn't a default** — you have to work around it.
- **Harder to use for one-off occasions.** It's built for ongoing family coordination, not a single birthday.

If you're managing a big extended family with multiple occasions per year and want one system, Giftster is reasonable. If you're a smaller group or care most about surprise, look elsewhere. It's the app most people search for as a "Giftster alternative" when they hit exactly this limitation.

## 5. Babylist — Best for Baby Registries

Babylist solved a specific problem beautifully: letting expectant parents register for items from multiple stores in one place. It's now the dominant baby registry app.

**What it does well:**

- **Any store, one registry.** Genuinely useful and the reason people switch to it.
- **Clean giver experience**, with a group gift feature that works for bigger purchases.
- **Must-have checklists** for baby prep, which new parents find useful.

**Where it falls short:**

- **It's a baby registry, not a general wishlist app.** Not designed for birthdays or holidays.
- **The recipient sees every purchase.** Standard registry model, and it spoils the surprise at the shower.
- **The iOS app is heavier than it needs to be.**

If you're having a baby, Babylist is probably the right tool. If you want a surprise shower — and some parents do, as I wrote in the [baby shower guide](/blog/baby-shower-surprise-gift-list/) — you'll want a parallel hidden-reservation list.

## 6. Zola — Best for Weddings

The dominant wedding registry platform, and for good reason. It handles the complexity of wedding registries — multiple stores, cash funds, experiences — in one place.

**What it does well:**

- **Wedding-specific features** like cash funds, honeymoon contributions, and experience gifts.
- **Elegant design**, which matters for the wedding aesthetic.
- **Givers can see availability and purchase directly.**

**Where it falls short:**

- **Wedding-only.** Doesn't work for birthdays or holidays.
- **The recipient sees every purchase.** The standard registry model. Some couples love that; some want the surprise of the shower, which I covered in the [wedding gift guide](/blog/wedding-gift-surprise/).

If you're registering for a wedding, Zola is a strong choice. It's not a general wishlist app.

## 7. The Notes App (Yes, Really)

Included because it's what a lot of people actually use, and it's worth acknowledging why.

**What it does well:**

- **Already on your phone.** Zero friction.
- **Complete flexibility.** Write whatever you want, in any format.
- **Private by default.** No accidental sharing.

**Where it falls short:**

- **Not shareable.** Screenshots lose structure and can't be updated.
- **No duplicate prevention, no group coordination.**
- **No surprise protection** because there's no reservation layer at all.

A fine place to _start_ a list — I recommend keeping a running note throughout the year, as I wrote in my post on [how to make a Christmas list](/blog/how-to-make-a-christmas-list/). Not a coordination tool.

## How to Choose the Right App for You

Match the app to the situation, not the other way around.

**If surprise matters most** — birthdays, holidays, showers where the recipient wants to be surprised — choose an app with genuine hidden reservations. Giftly Lists is built for this. The others aren't.

**If you're organizing a Secret Santa** — Elfster, and its exchange features are genuinely good.

**If you're registering for a wedding or a baby** — Zola and Babylist. They're designed for those registries and handle the complexity well.

**If you're managing a large extended family across many occasions** — Giftster is the most natural fit, though the design is dated.

**If you're just buying from Amazon anyway** — Amazon's wishlist is fine for that. Not a coordination tool, but it doesn't need to be.

**If you're coordinating a group gift** — use an app with contribution features. Giftly Lists and Elfster both do this, though Giftly Lists handles the surprise layer better. My [group gift coordination guide](/blog/group-gift-coordination-guide/) walks through the mechanics.

**If you want something free and shareable with zero setup** — a hidden-reservation list on Giftly Lists is free up to 3 lists, and givers don't need an account to view. That combination of free-plus-frictionless is the thing most apps get wrong.

## FAQ: Wishlist App Questions

**What's the best free wishlist app?**
Giftly Lists is free for up to 3 lists and doesn't require givers to create an account. Amazon's wishlist is also free but requires everyone to have an Amazon account and shows the recipient every purchase. Elfster is free but ad-supported.

**What's a good Giftster alternative?**
It depends on what you didn't like. If the problem was that Giftster shows the recipient reservations, Giftly Lists solves that with hidden reservations. If the problem was the dated interface, Elfster is more modern but weaker on surprise protection.

**What's the best wishlist app for couples or families?**
For couples managing multiple occasions across the year, Giftly Lists handles the range well — up to 25 lists on Pro. For large extended families with many households, Giftster is built for that specific structure.

**Can people view my wishlist without downloading an app?**
On Giftly Lists and Babylist, yes. On Amazon, Elfster, and Giftster, no — everyone needs an account. That matters more than people expect, because account requirements reduce participation, especially among older relatives.

**What's the best app for a shareable gift list?**
Giftly Lists, because the giver experience is a single link with no login. That's the definition of a shareable list — one that someone will actually open and use.

**What's the best wishlist app for a wedding?**
Zola, for the registry features. If you want a parallel surprise list for the shower, pair it with a hidden-reservation list so the couple doesn't see what's coming.

**What's the best app for a baby registry?**
Babylist, for the multi-store support. Again, pair it with a hidden-reservation list if you want the shower itself to have surprises.

**Is there a wishlist app that works on Android?**
Not Giftly Lists, which is iOS-only. Elfster and Giftster both have Android apps. Amazon's wishlist is available everywhere.

**Can you create a wishlist from multiple sites for free?**
Babylist and Zola both support multi-store items within their own registry formats. Giftly Lists lets you add items from any store by description or link, and it's free up to 3 lists. For a general-purpose multi-store wishlist, that's the most flexible free option.

**What's the best wishlist app for a Secret Santa?**
Elfster, primarily because the name draw and exclusions are built in. Giftly Lists handles the wishlist side better, so some groups use both.
